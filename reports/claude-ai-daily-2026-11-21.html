Anthropic Mathematician Says He and Claude Proved the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, said Friday that working with Claude he found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, the 1859 problem that carries a $1 million Clay Millennium Prize. He says it also covers the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis.

The proof was released only as Lean code, split into 14 packages and reportedly running past 250,000 pages. Alpöge says Claude supplied a key step extending an idea from Stepanov, and that he made the breakthrough late Sunday, Nov. 15, before clearing it with Dario Amodei.

Research

Top Mathematicians: Promising, Not Yet Verified

Fields Medalist James Maynard called it “clearly a historic milestone” if it fully checks out, and said he suspects the proof will turn out to be correct. Youness Lamzouri said he was “just in awe,” while warning that truly understanding it could take months or years.

Lean is machine-checkable, which is the whole point, but a quarter-million pages of it is hard for humans to read. Scientific American reports independent mathematicians now say the result is almost certainly true. Formal acceptance will take longer.

An Anthropic Engineer's Warning on “Comprehension Debt”

Yahoo Finance revisits Claude Code engineer Addy Osmani's essay on comprehension debt: AI can write code faster than teams can review it. His line: “the AI wrote it” won't hold up in a post-incident report when real assets are at stake.

The piece ties the idea to a run of high-profile outages. Practical takeaway for Claude Code users: keep review, tests and ownership in the loop, especially for anything that ships to prod.

Stanford Posts an All-Human Rival Proof, Then Hits Snags

Hours after Alpöge's post, AMS president Ravi Vakil shared an independent draft proof of GRH by Alessandro Masullo on his blog, stressing it was “all human, with no AI.” Brian Conrad had been reviewing it for months.

About eight hours later Vakil updated the post: several people found problems in Masullo's draft, many with help from AI models. Either way, AI ended up in the story.

A Bloomberg report says Apple is developing its own autonomous agent for device control, apps, browsing and coding, positioned against OpenAI and Anthropic. ANT and Meta slipped after hours Thursday on the news.

The Bear Case Shows Up: 18x Revenue and Rising Compute

The Motley Fool notes ANT trades around 18 times annualized revenue. Last quarter revenue grew 47% sequentially, but compute spending grew about 1.7 times as fast, flipping the company from its first operating profit to an operating loss.

Seaport Research started coverage at Neutral with no price target, citing slower Q3 growth. That's a contrast to the Buy calls from Melius and Needham earlier in the week.
