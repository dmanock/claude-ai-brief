📢 Official Updates
Corporate

Anthropic Commits 5% of Its Shares to a Public Benefit Fund

Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund in a Sunday blog post. It pledges 5% of the company’s shares with immediate effect, subject to stockholder approval, and adds 2% at the board’s discretion for every 50% rise in company value.

The fund can back nonprofits and direct distributions, including work on AI labor-market disruption. It cannot be used for lobbying or political donations. Al Jazeera notes Anthropic is preparing a possible record IPO before the end of 2026, with analysts forecasting a valuation above $2 trillion.

Research

Claude Designs Protein Binders for Scorpion Neurotoxins

Anthropic says an internal Claude research model, working mostly autonomously inside Claude Science over 64 hours, generated 318,484 protein designs aimed at five scorpion neurotoxins. A set of 211 was tested by Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience, and 17 met Anthropic’s binding criteria. The strongest Cn2 binders hit the single-digit nanomolar range.

Anthropic calls it a research demonstration, not an antivenom. Neutralization, safety and efficacy are unproven, and testing used recombinant toxins. All 211 designs and the binding data are being released for other researchers.

Policy

Amodei Answers Safety Critics in a 2,000-Word X Post

Responding to Encode’s Nathan Calvin, who accused Anthropic of weakening its safety commitments through $500 billion-plus in compute deals, growing reliance on Claude to build new models, and safety-policy changes, Dario Amodei listed nine commercially costly safety decisions. He said Anthropic was “the first, and still only, frontier company to restrict the sale of AI services to PRC-controlled companies.”

He acknowledged mistakes in how Anthropic communicated a draft federal AI safety bill and committed to propose options for regulating the pace of AI research by January 15, 2027, or explain why not. Fox News reports no response from Calvin or officials yet.

🧠 Analysis
Take

Safety Scrutiny Meets the IPO Runway

Two stories, one tension. Anthropic is defending its safety record in public while lining up what could be a record listing. The 5% fund is a clear gesture, but it is also a pre-IPO signal that the company wants its public-benefit story told on its terms.

Expect investors and Congress to ask the same question: does safety hold up under scale? Amodei’s January 15 promise gives critics a date to circle. Meanwhile the scorpion binder release shows the other side of the ledger, with careful caveats and open data.
