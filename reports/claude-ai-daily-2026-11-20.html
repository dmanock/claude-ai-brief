📢 Official Updates
Company

ANT Closes Its First Day Up 17% at $97.17

Anthropic's stock opened on the Nasdaq Global Select Market at $111.50 just before noon ET Thursday, 34% above the $83 IPO price, after Dario and Daniela Amodei rang the opening bell. It peaked at $115.10, slid to $92.72 by early afternoon and closed at $97.17, up 17%.

Roughly 857 million shares changed hands. At the close ANT was worth about $1.28 trillion on the prospectus share count, or roughly $1.7 trillion fully diluted. It kept about half of its $28.50 opening gain.

Company

Offering Closes Today as the Greenshoe Window Ends

The $81.3 billion offering is expected to close today, Nov. 20. Underwriters' option to buy up to 147 million more shares, worth about $12.2 billion at $83, is also exercisable until today.

If the option is fully used, the total raise would top $93 billion, putting even more distance between Anthropic and SpaceX's former record.

Claude Code

Claude Code 2.1.329 Shows Where Hooks Come From

The folder trust dialog now shows where a project's hooks are declared before they run, so you can see what you're approving. /teleport to a cloud session now starts a new session, like /clear, instead of replacing your open conversation, and /resume brings the old one back.

On the MCP side, connection errors from remote HTTP servers now appear in the Server message row of /mcp, and Claude Code reconnects most HTTP MCP servers still on an older protocol version automatically.

SaetaIA GitHub release
Claude Code

No More Mystery .bin Files From MCP Servers

Also in 2.1.329: PDFs, images and .docx, .xlsx and .pptx files sent by MCP servers or the web as application/octet-stream are no longer saved as .bin. They keep a usable file type.

The release also bundles a batch of fixes across sessions, MCP and Code Mode. If you lean on MCP tools that return documents, this one is worth updating for.

Markets

IPO Buyers Made About $14 Billion on Day One

Yahoo Finance's chart of the day puts the paper gain for ANT's IPO buyers at roughly $14 billion, second only to SpaceX's $14.4 billion in June and well ahead of Alibaba and Visa.

The catch: ANT's 13% slide from its first trade to the close was steeper than all but one of 20 big US IPOs since 2008. Only Coupang faded harder.

Markets

Leveraged ANT ETFs Start Trading Monday

Defiance's 2x long (ANTC) and 2x short (ANTQ) single-stock ETFs on Anthropic begin trading Monday. That lands right as listed ANT options are expected to arrive.

Leveraged products plus options in week one means more volatility, in both directions, for a stock that already swung more than $22 on its first day.

Protests and a Senator's Warning Shadow the Debut

Protesters gathered outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters on Thursday. Last month Sen. Elizabeth Warren wrote to Dario Amodei warning the IPO could bring volatility and risk to retail investors.

Meanwhile OpenAI is reportedly targeting a 2027 listing, and Sam Altman told Semafor that demand for AI shares far outstrips supply.

Markets

Friday Premarket: ANT Slips About 6%

ANT traded around $90.50 in early Friday premarket, down roughly 6% from Thursday's close but still above the $83 IPO price. The broader tape is the bigger story: the Nasdaq fell 2.25% Thursday, Nvidia is on a six-day losing streak, and futures pointed lower again.

Dan Ives called the debut a big near-term win for the tech sector. The market seems to be saying the AI trade needs more than one blockbuster listing to turn around.

Google Ships Gemini 4 Neon the Same Week

Google launched Gemini 4 Neon on Nov. 19 at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output, with a 1 million token context window. It's rolling out in the Gemini API, AI Studio and the Gemini app.

Take

Day One Was a Vote on AI, Not Just on Claude

ANT held a 17% gain on a day the Nasdaq dropped more than 2% and the AI trade cracked. Stripped of the macro mood, that's a solid open for the biggest IPO ever.

The real test starts now. Options, leveraged ETFs, a Dec. 15 lock-up and the first quarterly print as a public company will decide whether a trillion-plus valuation holds. Meanwhile the dev side keeps shipping, which is the part that actually earns the number.
