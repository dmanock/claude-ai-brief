Engineering

Scion: Anthropic Replaces Git in Its Monorepo

Anthropic built Scion, a server-centric, API-first source control system, in about five months with seven engineers. Daily merges rose from roughly 2,500 in May to more than 30,000 in September as agents took over the workload. CI alone runs about 25 million checkouts a day.

Anthropic claims about 200x less CPU and memory than its old Git mirrors, with checkouts dropping from minutes to seconds. It says Scion is not a suitable general Git replacement and will not be released. Claude helped build it, including a pipeline rewrite that ran 4x faster on 20x less memory.

Takeaway

What Scion Means for Agent-Heavy Teams

The lesson is not “ditch Git.” Anthropic urges the Git community to improve server-side features, RPCs and negotiation protocols instead. But if agents open PRs around the clock, always-online design and regional caches start to matter.

Worth a look if your CI checkout time is creeping up: bounded local caches, no packfile maintenance, and sparse checkouts are the ideas to steal.

Policy

Labs Reportedly Pushed to Control Evaluator Publications

Reuters reports Anthropic and OpenAI sought substantial control over what outside evaluators could publish, citing trade secrets and the risk of embedded reviewers carrying information to rivals. Both wanted strict control through at least October 5.

Anthropic says reviewers it has worked with before decide their own methods and findings. The reports land awkwardly beside Dario Amodei's public stance that evaluators should publish main findings without company edits.
