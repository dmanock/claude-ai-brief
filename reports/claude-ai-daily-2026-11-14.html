Company

Banks Reportedly Bar China and Hong Kong Orders From the IPO

Several banks on Anthropic's IPO are rejecting buy orders from investors in mainland China and Hong Kong, or calling allocations unlikely, according to Bloomberg. Some cite ITAR, the US export-control rules for defense-related goods. Anthropic declined to comment.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the deal with 20 other banks. It lines up with this week's region policy, which already restricts Claude use from China, Hong Kong and Macau. Pricing is expected Nov. 18, with ANT trading on Nasdaq Nov. 19.

Company

Amodei Calls Compute His Deepest Business Dilemma

On Friday's roadshow livestream with JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon, Dario Amodei named securing compute as Anthropic's deepest business dilemma and pitched investors on underpromising and overdelivering. He also said he turns down brilliant jerks, spends 40 to 50% of his time on talent and culture, and that all seven co-founders are still there.

The book is reportedly about six times oversubscribed, and underwriters may price above the $70 to $78 range. Filings show roughly $900 billion in future compute commitments against more than $95 billion in cash as of Sept. 30.

Platform

Self-Hosted Claude Managed Agents Enters Private Beta

Anthropic now lets companies run the entire Claude Managed Agents service on their own infrastructure, in their cloud or on-prem. That goes beyond May's self-hosted sandboxes: you host the agents, credentials and session history, and choose where model requests go.

Requests can route to Anthropic, Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry or your own gateway, with per-environment host allowlists and OpenTelemetry records per session. Access is through your Anthropic account team. Pricing hasn't been published.

Claude Code

Claude Code 2.1.323 Adds an Experimental Code Mode

The new opt-in Code Mode has Claude write JavaScript to call its tools instead of making one tool call at a time. It's experimental, so expect rough edges.

Plugin authors get an engines field in plugin.json for version gating, and claude plugin validate now type-checks hooks. Long hooks in headless and SDK runs send a heartbeat every 30 seconds, and Artifact uploads now accept .docx.

Claude Code

Claude Code 2.1.325 Tightens the Security Defaults

Sandboxed Bash now goes through the normal permission flow by default, unless you set sandbox.autoAllowBashIfSandboxed. Read-only git commands in untrusted repos ask first, and scheduled tasks and /loop ask before running a skill that declares allowed-tools or hooks.

It also patches zstd response decoding and lets sessions on many third-party gateways and Foundry compact instead of failing on context length. If your automations relied on the old auto-allow, check them after updating.

Industry

Claude for Manufacturing Launches

Anthropic's new manufacturing plugin bundles 17 skills for jobs like quoting, failed-inspection investigations, breakdown diagnosis and production reporting. It ships with connectors for Autodesk Fusion, Infor, Microsoft 365 and Oracle NetSuite.

Any Claude enterprise customer can install it. Anthropic says a person signs off on every draft and that more skills will be added over time.

Policy

White House Task Force Pushes AI Incident Reporting

At a White House meeting on Thursday, the administration's AI task force recommended that agencies ease cyber-liability policies to encourage AI incident reports, according to The Hinge newsletter.

Members also heard that signers of the White House AI accord, which includes Anthropic and OpenAI, are preparing a common set of industry standards. Details on those standards haven't been released.

🧠 Analysis
Take

A 6x Book Buys Time, Not Chips

Amodei calling compute the hard part is the most honest line of the roadshow. Roughly $900 billion in future compute commitments against $95 billion-plus in cash means the IPO is less a finish line than a refill. Underpromising makes sense when your biggest bill is the next model.

The China order rejections point the same way. Anthropic is pricing itself as a national-security-adjacent company: picky about customers, picky about shareholders. That costs some demand, but with a book this deep it barely registers.
