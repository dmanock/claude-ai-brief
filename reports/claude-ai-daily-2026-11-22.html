Lean FRO and Mathlib Back the Claude-Assisted Riemann Proof

On Saturday the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative issued a joint statement backing Levent Alpöge's Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis as correct. Per the statement, the Lean kernel, con-leche, con-ron and third-party checkers all accepted the exported proof.

That's a big step from Friday, when the claim was only days old and checks were still running. The Lean code runs about 17 million lines across 14 archives. Anthropic hadn't commented formally as of Saturday.

A Nov. 20 update on Releasebot's Claude feed says computer use is now in beta for Team and Enterprise plans. Claude can see and work in allowed desktop apps, and the rollout is gradual across Claude Desktop, web and mobile.

How the Proof Actually Got Checked

Waterloo's Michael Rubinstein reports his own check finished clean: all 175,187 modules compile, the five main theorems rely only on Lean's three standard axioms, the kernel replay accepted everything, and the nanoda checker verified 1,484,729 declarations with no errors.

Independent runs by Andrew Sutherland and others matched. The full export unpacks to about 61.6 GB, which says a lot about how much compute machine-checked math now takes.

What's Still Missing: A Paper Humans Can Read

A 165-page working draft explaining the zeta-function argument in human terms appeared Saturday. That matters: no readable paper existed when the claim went public, and mathematicians want to understand the proof, not just trust the checker.

Fine print: the generalized result covers Dirichlet L-functions. The $1 million Clay prize needs publication plus two years of scrutiny, so late 2028 at the earliest. Polymarket had just 2% odds on a Clay declaration this year.
